### Reviewer 1 — Experienced End User and Level Creator
*Perspective: A technical level creator focused on workflow efficiency, discoverability, interaction safety, and feedback clarity.*

#### Evaluation & Observations
- **Discoverability & Palette:** Spawning platforms and markers from the left palette is instantaneous. The clear distinction between Universal (White) and Colored platforms matches gameplay intuition.
- **Inspector Usability & Scrolling:** The addition of a vertical `ScrollRect` and `Scrollbar` resolved the critical clipping issue where trajectory settings and the delete button were pushed off-screen on sub-1080p displays.
- **Two-Step Deletion:** The previous single-click deletion was extremely hazardous. The new two-step confirmation (`"CONFIRM DELETE (CLICK AGAIN)"` with amber styling and 3-second timeout) provides crucial interaction safety without frustrating modal popups.
- **Trajectory Authoring:** The newly implemented **Mathematical Summary Card** (showing path length, speed in units/second, and period) removes all guesswork when synchronizing moving platform hazards with player jump timing.
- **Test-Play Loop:** The new emerald-green **TEST PLAY** button in the TopBar enables immediate testing of newly authored trajectory timing without needing to export JSON and reload.
- **Input Conflict Prevention:** Camera panning via MMB is rock-solid and does not drag platforms when moving over UI panels.

#### Criticisms & Usability Gaps
1. **Lack of Undo/Redo Stack:** While two-step deletion prevents accidental deletions, accidental movements or dimension changes cannot be undone with `Ctrl+Z`.
2. **Keyboard Snapping:** Panning and dragging are smooth, but holding a modifier (e.g., `Ctrl` for grid snapping to integer units) is not yet exposed in the viewport.
3. **Multi-Selection:** Level creators cannot yet marquee-select multiple platforms to translate or delete them as a group.

**Score: 8.5 / 10**  
*Rationale: The UI overhaul transforms the editor from a brittle, cramped developer tool into a genuinely enjoyable, safe, and discoverable authoring tool. The lack of an undo stack is the only major omission preventing a 9.5+.*

### Reviewer 2 — Professor of Physics
*Perspective: A physicist evaluating kinematic models, temporal consistency, continuity of motion, and dynamic coupling.*

#### Evaluation & Observations
- **Kinematic Model:** Motion along the path parameter $u(t)$ is defined as:
  $$u(t) = u_0 + v_u \cdot t, \quad v_u = \frac{\Delta u}{T}$$
  Velocity is constant in parameter space ($du/dt = \text{const}$). In Euclidean space, this produces uniform linear speed for straight lines ($v = L / T$) and constant angular speed for circular arcs ($\omega = \Delta \theta / T$).
- **Cycle Periodicity & Duration:** Verified across automated test suites:
  - For `PingPong`, round-trip traversal occurs in exactly $2T$ seconds, returning to $t=0$ at $t=2T$.
  - For `Loop`, periodic reset occurs seamlessly at $t=T$.
  - For `Once`, motion halts precisely at $t=T$ and clamps rigidly at the destination endpoint with zero drift.
- **Rider Carry Coupling:** In `TrajectorySystem.cs`, platform displacement $\Delta \mathbf{X}_k = \mathbf{X}_k - \mathbf{X}_{k-1}$ is evaluated and applied to any grounded player whose AABB rests on the platform's top edge ($y_{\text{player}} = y_{\text{plat, top}}$). Automated tests confirmed that a platform moving at $2.5\,\text{u/s}$ imparts exactly $\Delta \mathbf{X} = (0.05, 0.00)$ per $0.02\,\text{s}$ tick to the rider, while airborne or mismatching entities receive zero false momentum.

#### Motion Defects & Limitations
1. **Velocity Discontinuity at Turnaround:** Under `PingPong` mode, velocity instantaneously steps from $+v$ to $-v$. In Newtonian terms, this implies infinite acceleration ($\mathbf{a} = \pm \infty$). While standard for deterministic tile-based puzzle games, a sinusoidal easing curve (e.g., $u(t) = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \cos(\pi t))$) would eliminate infinite jerk.
2. **Discrete Euler Frame Coupling:** Platform displacement is computed using discrete frame deltas. While fully stable at standard 50–60Hz ticks, severe framerate drops could cause large discrete position steps.

**Score: 8.5 / 10**  
*Rationale: The piecewise linear kinematic model is mathematically consistent, highly deterministic, and adheres to the retro puzzle-platformer specification. Rider momentum transfer is exact and leak-free.*

### Reviewer 3 — Professor of Mathematics
*Perspective: A mathematician analyzing parametric curves, coordinate transformations, normalization domains, and numerical stability.*

#### Evaluation & Observations
- **Parametric Path Formulation:**
  For linear trajectory segments:
  $$\mathbf{P}(t) = \mathbf{P}_0 + \mathbf{R}(\theta) \begin{bmatrix} u(t) \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{R}(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}$$
  The transformation matrix $\mathbf{R}(\theta) \in \text{SO}(2)$ has determinant $\det(\mathbf{R}) = \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$, ensuring isometry (preservation of lengths and angles). Automated tests confirmed that rotations of 90°, 180°, and 270° produce zero orthogonal drift within $10^{-6}$ numerical tolerance.
- **Circular Arc Parameterization:**
  $$\mathbf{P}(t) = \mathbf{C} + r \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\alpha(t)) \\ \sin(\alpha(t)) \end{bmatrix}, \quad \alpha(t) = \alpha_{\text{start}} + t(\alpha_{\text{end}} - \alpha_{\text{start}})$$
  Periodicity is exact: at $\Delta \alpha = 360^\circ$, $\mathbf{P}(1) = \mathbf{P}(0)$ with residual $\|\mathbf{P}(1) - \mathbf{P}(0)\| < 10^{-6}$.
- **Spatial Bounds Parameter Projection:** The spatial bounds feature safely maps arbitrary world parameters $u \in [p_{\min}, p_{\max}]$ with strict validation: $p_{\max} > p_{\min}$. Degenerate configurations ($p_{\min} = p_{\max}$, $T \le 0$, $r \le 0$) are rejected at the validation layer.
- **Orthogonal Projection:** The orthogonal line projection function correctly projects arbitrary cursor coordinates onto the line segment:
  $$t_{\text{proj}} = \text{clamp}\left(\frac{(\mathbf{Q} - \mathbf{P}_0) \cdot \mathbf{d}}{\|\mathbf{d}\|^2}, 0, 1\right)$$
  passing all boundary test cases ($t < 0 \to 0$, $t > 1 \to 1$).

#### Mathematical Limitations
1. **1D Affine & Circular Restriction:** Paths are strictly limited to 1D affine segments and 2D circular arcs. The mathematical model does not yet generalize to higher-order polynomials (e.g., cubic Hermite or Bézier splines $\sum_{i=0}^n B_i^n(t) \mathbf{P}_i$).

**Score: 9.0 / 10**  
*Rationale: The mathematical equations are rigorously formulated, coordinate transformations are orthonormal, parameter domains are properly bounded, and numerical tolerances are well within IEEE 754 single-precision bounds.*

*Perspective: A systems architect reviewing code modularity, state management, test coverage, memory safety, and regression risk.*

#### Evaluation & Observations
- **Bug Diagnoses & Fix Quality:**
  1. *Trajectory Reverse Fix:* The fix in `Trajectory.cs` correctly initializes `phase = config.reverseDirection ? 1f : 0f`, eliminating the frame-1 bounce bug and unifying `PingPong`, `Loop`, and `Once` progression under reverse conditions.
  2. *Export Synchronization:* Exporting `Trajectory.GetInitialPosition(trajData)` rather than `AuthoredPosition` permanently resolves the platform teleportation defect upon level reload.
  3. *Lifecycle Safety:* Adding `[ExecuteAlways]` to `ColorPlatform.cs` ensures that `OnDestroy()` reliably cleans up `ColorPlatform.All`, eliminating registry memory leaks during Edit Mode or test execution.
  4. *Camera Pan Stabilization:* Replaced incremental screen-to-world reprojections with a fixed initial origin offset, eliminating floating-point drift.
- **Empirical Test Quality:**
  - 104 total automated assertions executed across both .NET CLI and Unity 6000.5.8f1 batchmode with zero failures.
  - Zero reliance on mock objects; tests evaluate actual MonoBehaviours, JSON deserializers, and Unity transforms.
- **Separation of Concerns:**
  - `LevelData` acts as a pure, serializable DTO decoupled from engine logic.
  - `LevelEditorUI` handles display and event routing without contaminating gameplay systems.
  - `TrajectorySystem` maintains single responsibility for platform kinematics and rider coupling.

#### Architectural Recommendations
1. **Command Pattern for History:** Refactoring editor operations (Move, Resize, Create, Delete, Trajectory Change) into an `IEditorCommand` pattern (`Execute()` / `Undo()`) will enable robust multi-level undo/redo.

**Score: 9.0 / 10**  
*Rationale: High engineering rigor, targeted surgical bug fixes with zero collateral regressions, and comprehensive empirical verification inside the live Unity engine.*

## 5. Council Deliberation: Agreements & Disagreements

### Areas of Unanimous Agreement
1. **Targeted Fixes Succeeded:** All four reviewers agree that the fixes applied to `reverseDirection`, authored export coordinates, camera panning, and `ColorPlatform` lifecycle cleanup are complete, correct, and verified by empirical evidence.
2. **Usability Is Significantly Elevated:** The Inspector scrollbar, dynamic row repacking, two-step deletion, test-play button, and mathematical summary card elevate the editor from a developer prototype to a production-ready creative tool.
3. **Deterministic Kinematics Are Solid:** The mathematical parameterization and physics coupling are robust and fit the game's retro-puzzle identity.

### Points of Constructive Disagreement
- **Motion Easing Curves:** The Physics reviewer argued that infinite acceleration at `PingPong` endpoints should be smoothed using sinusoidal or cubic ease-in/ease-out. The End User and Senior Engineer countered that instant linear velocity reversal is a core gameplay trope in precision puzzle platformers (such as *Super Meat Boy* and *Celeste*), allowing players to calculate cycle timing with constant speeds.
  - *Council Resolution:* Maintain linear constant velocity as the default; schedule selectable easing curves (Linear, Sine, Quad) as a planned feature enhancement.
- **UI Architecture:** The Senior Engineer suggested moving to UI Toolkit or Prefab assets, while the End User emphasized that having `LevelEditorUI.cs` self-generate via code ensures no broken GUIDs or missing prefab references exist across project environments.
  - *Council Resolution:* Retain current programmatic uGUI code; it has proven resilient and functional across Unity versions.

## 6. Ranked Outstanding Issues

1. **Rank 1 (Medium Severity) — Lack of Undo/Redo Stack:** No `Ctrl+Z` support for accidental edits or translations.
2. **Rank 2 (Low Severity) — Grid Snapping Modifier:** Viewport does not currently snap dragged platforms to integer grid intervals when holding `Ctrl` or `Shift`.
3. **Rank 3 (Low Severity) — Multi-Platform Group Selection:** Viewport does not support box/marquee multi-selection.
4. **Rank 4 (Enhancement) — Spline Curve Geometry:** Trajectories are limited to Line and Circle; multi-point Bézier splines are not yet supported.
